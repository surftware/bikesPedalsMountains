Bike Race Safety Rules

Mountain biking is an inherently risky activity. These Safety Rules are not a guarantee against injury or death to riders, spectators, or others. However, you can significantly reduce your risk by riding with skill and awareness, using high-quality protective gear, maintaining your equipment properly, and reporting unsafe conditions or behaviour to race officials.

Riders are solely responsible for ensuring that their bicycles are fit for competition—capable of withstanding the increased demands of high-speed descents, sudden braking, unpredictable terrain, and close proximity to other competitors.

Every bike must be inspected prior to the event, and some races may require a signed Technical Inspection Form.

Participants are reminded that meeting the minimum safety standards does not equal optimal protection. It is strongly recommended that riders use gear—such as helmets, pads, and protective clothing—that exceeds the minimum requirements.

Riders competing in more advanced or aggressive categories, such as downhill or enduro, are encouraged to wear full-face helmets, back protectors, and other certified safety equipment.

Your safety is a shared responsibility. Take it seriously—for your sake and for everyone else on the trail.

Each rider is expected to:

Equipment inspection

Inspect equipment before each race, ensuring:

  • Proper brake function, front and rear.
  • Securely fastened wheels.
  • Intact drivetrain and suspension components.

Self-reliance

Be self-reliant, which includes:

  • Carrying basic repair tools, such as a tube, pump and multi-tool.
  • Knowing basic first aid procedures.

Race protocols

Respect all race protocols, including:

  • Attending pre-race briefings.
  • Following start procedures and assigned wave times.
  • Understanding the event course and hazard markings.

Helmets

Helmets must meet one of the following certifications:

  • CPSC — United States.
  • EN 1078 — European Union.
  • ASTM F1952 — Downhill-specific certification.

The helmet must:

  • Fit snugly and fasten securely.
  • Be worn at all times while riding.

Protective Gear

The following equipment is strongly recommended for all riders and mandatory for youth and downhill events:

  • Full-finger gloves are preferred.
  • Eye protection, such as goggles or glasses.
  • Knee and elbow pads.

For Enduro and Downhill races:

  • Full-face helmets are mandatory.
  • Chest and back protection is recommended.

Bike Standards

All bikes must:

  • Be appropriate for the terrain and competition class, such as hardtail or full-suspension.
  • Contain no motorized assistance unless:
    • The category explicitly allows Class 1 e-MTBs.
    • The e-bike follows the established maximum wattage limit, usually 250 W or less.
    • It uses pedal assistance only and has no throttle.

On-Course Behavior

  • Stay within marked trail boundaries.
  • Yield according to the following rules:
    • Always yield to emergency vehicles and personnel.
    • Uphill riders have the right-of-way on shared-use trails during non-race days.
    • Slower riders must allow overtaking when it is safe.

Prohibited behaviors include:

  • Cutting switchbacks.
  • Deliberate obstruction.
  • Verbal or physical aggression.

Passing Protocol

Overtaking must be:

  • Verbally announced, for example: Rider on your left!
  • Executed with consideration and adequate space.
  • Performed only when trail conditions permit it.

Lapped riders must yield at the first safe opportunity.

Race Modifications

The Race Director reserves the right to:

  • Delay or reschedule start times.
  • Alter course segments for safety.
  • Cancel an event because of:
    • Severe weather, including lightning, hail or extreme heat.
    • Hazardous trail conditions, including flooding or landslides.

Rider Responsibilities in Bad Weather

  • Bring appropriate clothing layers and hydration.
  • Protect electronic devices during rain or cold conditions.
  • Recognize the signs of hypothermia and heatstroke.

Crash Protocol

If a rider is injured, the first rider on scene must:

  • Stop and assess the safety of the scene.
  • Notify a marshal or call the emergency number displayed on the race plate.
  • Remain at the scene if the injured rider is immobile or unconscious.

Medical Evaluation

A rider must be evaluated by medical personnel if they:

  • Have sustained a head injury.
  • Were unconscious.
  • Show signs of serious trauma.

The rider must be cleared by medical personnel before continuing.

Substance Policy

There is zero tolerance for:

  • Alcohol before or during competition.
  • Performance-enhancing drugs.
  • Recreational drug use.

Behavior Standards

Grounds for disqualification include:

  • Abusive language.
  • Disrespecting officials.
  • Tampering with course markings.
  • Littering on the trail.

Race officials may:

  • Issue warnings or penalties.
  • Enforce immediate disqualification.
  • Remove unsafe riders from the course.

All decisions made by the Race Director are final.

By registering, riders agree that:

  • They have read, understood and will comply with all safety regulations.
  • Participation is voluntary and all inherent risks are accepted.
